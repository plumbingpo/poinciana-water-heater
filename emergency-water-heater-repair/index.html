How to Tell Something Is Wrong

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • The relief valve on the side of the tank dripping, hissing, or discharging
  • A heater breaker that will not stay on after being reset
  • Water at the tap that is suddenly scalding hot
  • Sudden loud knocking or boiling sounds coming from inside the tank
  • The smell of gas near the unit, a situation that calls for evacuating and phoning the utility or 911
  • Scorch marks, soot, or flames rolling out near the burner access on a gas unit
  • Standing water around the water heater that returns after you wipe it up
  • A complete loss of hot water throughout the house

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

It is tempting to treat a leaking or dead water heater as a quick fix. In reality, you may be dealing with standing water beside 240-volt wiring, a gas supply and open burner a few inches from a puddle, and a tank of very hot water under pressure. The relief valve, if it is firing, is the final line of defense between an overheated tank and a violent failure, and plugging it is one of the most dangerous things anyone can do to a heater. Relighting a pilot while gas lingers, or forcing a rusted connection, can make things much worse. Evacuate for gas, isolate, then phone is the right sequence for anyone without the tools and training.

Professional help is not just about having wrenches. It is about knowing why the failure happened so it does not repeat a few weeks later. When a relief valve discharges, we measure static water pressure and look for a missing or failed expansion tank. When a tank floods the floor, we locate the true source, whether it is the valve, a fitting, a connector, or the tank wall. When a gas heater dies, we check thermocouple or flame sensor signal, gas pressure, and venting before restarting it. The diagnosis determines everything that follows. Components can be replaced the same visit; a failed tank cannot be patched, and we say so plainly and explain the replacement path.

How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Here's where to begin:

  • Know where the heater shutoff and the main water valve are, and make sure everyone in the house does too
  • Leave clear space around a gas heater and never store flammable liquids nearby
  • A simple water sensor on the floor beside the tank can warn you hours earlier
  • Drain a few gallons or flush the tank annually to keep sediment from cooking the tank floor
  • Test the T&P valve annually; a valve that sticks or weeps afterward should be replaced
  • A drain pan with a proper drain line under the tank can turn a flood into a manageable puddle
  • Set the temperature around 120F, which protects against burns and slows tank wear
