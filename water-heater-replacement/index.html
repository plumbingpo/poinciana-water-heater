When to Call for Water Heater Replacement

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • Hot water running out faster every month
  • Loud rumbling or popping that flushing no longer fixes
  • A heater older than most tanks last, usually eight to twelve years
  • Energy bills rising while hot water gets harder to come by
  • Repairs that keep coming back, one part after another
  • Rust-colored hot water while the cold stays clear
  • Water seeping out from under the tank jacket
  • Rust flaking from the tank jacket or its connections

Why Hire a Professional for Water Heater Replacement?

Swapping a water heater looks simple until you count the connections. A gas unit needs a leak-free gas connection with a sediment trap, a vent that is sized and pitched correctly so carbon monoxide goes outside, and combustion air it can actually reach. An electric unit needs a circuit and breaker matched to the element wattage. Every tank needs a correctly rated relief valve with a discharge pipe routed to a safe spot, and many homes also need an expansion tank, a drain pan, or seismic straps under local code. Going tankless or switching to a heat pump adds even more: bigger gas lines, different vent materials, dedicated circuits, and condensate drains.

Professional replacement gets all of it right the first time. Our experienced technicians size the heater to your household, verify the gas line, vent, and electrical service, and install whatever code items apply. We leak-test, check draft and carbon monoxide on gas units, and set a safe temperature before we leave. That is the difference between a heater that runs quietly for a decade and one that starts with a problem on day one. And when a repair would give your current tank several more good years, we say so instead of selling you a new one.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Here are our recommendations:

  • Keep the space around a heat pump model clear so it can draw heat from the room
  • Set the thermostat around 120 degrees to prevent scalding and reduce wear
  • Write the install date on the tank so you know when to plan the next replacement
  • Start a flushing routine in the first year, before sediment hardens
  • Keep household water pressure in a safe range to protect the tank and its fittings
  • A fresh anode rod at the right time is the cheapest way to extend tank life
  • Tankless units need a yearly descaling, especially where the water is hard
