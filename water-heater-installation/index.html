How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Plans to change fuel types or go all-electric
  • Missing code items like an expansion tank, drain pan, or proper venting
  • New construction or a major renovation that needs a heater from scratch
  • Gallons wasted down the drain while you wait for hot water
  • Remodel plans that add fixtures or a second bathroom
  • A large soaking tub that a standard tank cannot fill
  • Hot water running out before everyone has showered
  • A water heater installed above finished rooms with no drain pan

Why Hire a Professional for Water Heater Installation?

Installing a new water heater means doing several trades' work at once. Gas piping has to be sized for the heater's input and every other appliance on the line, then pressure-tested and leak-free. The vent must meet the manufacturer's rules on diameter, length, pitch, and clearances, or carbon monoxide can find its way indoors. Electric and heat pump units need the right circuit, breaker, and wire gauge, and a whole-house electric tankless unit can demand more current than many panels can spare. Then come the water-side details: shutoffs, dielectric transitions, a correctly rated relief valve with a full-size discharge line, and code items like expansion tanks, drain pans, and seismic straps that differ from place to place.

Professional installation gets every piece right the first time. Our experienced technicians size the heater, calculate the gas line, lay out the vent route, confirm electrical capacity, and add the code items your location calls for. We test for leaks, check draft and carbon monoxide on gas units, and leave the heater at a safe temperature with the paperwork in order. That is the difference between a heater that simply works for years and one that fails inspection or backdrafts on the first cold morning. Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Here's where to begin:

  • Drain a few gallons from a new tank twice a year to control sediment
  • Have the anode rod inspected every few years to protect the tank
  • A 120-degree setting protects against burns and slows mineral buildup
  • Plan an annual descaling if you installed a tankless unit
  • Keep flammable liquids and clutter away from gas heaters
  • Moisture in the drain pan or at the relief line is an early warning worth a call
  • Leave working space around the heater, and never stack storage against it
