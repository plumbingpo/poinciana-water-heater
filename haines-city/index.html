Keeping Your Haines City Water Heater Running Longer

Most premature tank failures trace back to maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Flush the tank once a year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and test the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Set the thermostat close to 120 degrees for safer water and less scale. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year to protect the heat exchanger.

Equipment age matters just as much. With regular maintenance, a standard tank typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like rust-colored water, popping noises, or moisture around the base, it's time to plan a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and causes far less stress than an emergency swap after a leak.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Consider a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a short, inexpensive repair, but once it's gone, the water begins corroding the tank itself, and just like that a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and call at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and prevents a flooded utility room.
